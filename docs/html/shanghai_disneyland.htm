Resort News - (1/1/2026) Now that 2026 is here, Disney has confirmed that the Shanghai Disney Resort will turn 10 this year, and offers up a peek at the new 10th Anniversary logo for the resort on social media along with the Shanghai Resort’s new tagline for the year “With You, It’s Magic+”
    While most mainstream audiences will simply see the “+” symbol at the end as short of nod towards Disney’s consumer streaming products like Disney+ and ESPN+, Disney was quick to note that the “+” symbol also represents the Chinese character for the number ten.
    As you might expect, Disney is working on lots of fun ways to celebrate the anniversary in the park over the coming year, so more on that as it gets revealed. Currently the park is finishing up the “Disney Winter Frostival” that will take place through to March 3rd, 2026, ahead of the actual anniversary date of the grand opening which happened back on June 16, 2016.

    (4/27/2024) Shanghai Disneyland is apparently looking to build a rather large new roller coaster that will feature forward and backwards launch sections. A layout drawing was discovered within government permits filed it seems and has been reposted on social media. From the look of things the trains will move into some kind of scene area and stop, only to be launched right into the backwards section of the ride first, before coming to a stop within another ride building where it will then launch forward into the much longer forward facing ride portion.
    All said, the attraction is said to occupy a 5 to 6 acre site and appears to be a mostly outdoor coaster with a few indoor sections, mainly to enclose the two reverse and launch points, as well as the station. The theme of the coaster is not known at this time, it will be located on a currently unused expansion plot tucked right in between the new Zootopia themed land and the backstage ride-building for the Pirates of the Carribbean attraction. The way things are laid out right now on the layout, the access point to the new coaster would be through Zootopia, which could also make this the most logical theme for the new attraction as well and give the land a second phase expansion.
    Of course, this could also be some kind of stand-alone themed attraction as well, much like Big Grizzly Mountain roller coaster at Hong Kong Disneyland, which also features forward and backwards sections, and a launch. In fact, the layout of the section of the new Shanghai coaster where it rolls backwards into an enclosed area to allow for the track to switch before it launches forward once again along a parallel track is essentially a mirror-image version of how it happens on the Vekoma built Big Grizzly Mountain in Hong Kong Disneyland (photo). The basic layout of the coaster, which features a lot of spiraling turns and cross over points is also reminiscent of a Big Thunder / Big Grizzly style mine-train roller coaster, which would be considered a staple check-box experience offered by every single one of Disney’s main “Disneyland” style theme parks except for Shanghai right now. Much like Hong Kong, Shanghai Disneyland was also built without any kind of “Frontierland” style land, so a stand-alone coaster like Big Grizzly would also fit well to serve that function.

???? - Second Theme Park / “Project Atlas” - In Development - (5/23/2026) An interesting social media post caught my eye this week. Last month the name “Project Atlas” was leaked into the wild as a rumored internal project name that WDI may be using to develop a second theme park for the Shanghai Disney resort property. According to the new social media post, a leaked Chinese government document (not shown) apparently confirms that there is a 810,000 m² site reserved for use to add a second theme park.
    This itself is not unusual… as Disney has routinely future engineered their new theme park resorts in a way to leave plots available for use to add future hotels, retail developments and room for a second and even a third possible theme park. This has come to light in the last several years, as it was revealed that the Hong Kong government reclaimed the site once reserved for that resort’s second theme park during the COVID pandemic to install a large number of quarantine facility buildings. Apparently Disney had a long term option with the government to “save” that land for future development, but without being asked to buy it. Apparently, after delaying the idea of adding a second park for far too long, the government apparently opted not to renew Disney’s option to use the land and built the “Penny’s Bay Community Isolation Facility” on the site instead. I’m  not sure what it is used for now, or if Disney may have a future option to use it, but for now a second park in Hong Kong is off the table.
    Meanwhile it was revealed a couple of years ago that Disney has another land option deal at the Disneyland Paris resort for a possible future 3rd theme park. The terms for use here are interesting however, as it was revealed back in 2020 that the agreement between the local government and Disney to commit to using the property was going to be extended by several years. The new deadline is 2036 for Disney to commit to using the property in Marne-la-Vallee, however… as part of the extension there is now a special requirement in place by the government that the attendance at the current theme park resort must rise to serve at least 22 million guests a year by the 2036 deadline. Clearly this was the incentive that Disney needed in order to push forward with the current expansion of the resorts second park, which was just rebranded as Disney Adventure World.
    So what could Disney be planning to construct as the theme of a second theme park in Shanghai? According to the post, there was once an “EPCOT-of-the-East” plan, but they say that has been scrapped, which doesn’t surprise me or anyone else that remember that long before California Adventure was built Disney has also envisioned “WESTCOT” as the theme of the second California park. Instead Shanghai could be borrowing some ideas Disney has been throwing around for a park with an Avatar themed land, and could very well borrow some ideas from the Disney Adventure World design book, and other popular additions seen elsewhere in the Disney empire. The post seems to think that Disney could possibly confirm at least the initial plans and existence of Project Atlas this summer, possibly at D23 or at a future quarterly investment call.
