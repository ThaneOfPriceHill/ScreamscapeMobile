General Resort News - (7/17/2026) For the first time in three years, the Tokyo Disney Resort reports that they will be raising ticket prices this October. The maximum price for a one-day ticket to visit one of the two theme parks will rise 1,500 yen, which is approximately $9.30 US, to 12,400 yen.
    Keep in mind that the Tokyo Disney Resort now uses dynamic pricing, so ticket prices can vary from day to day, and even go up on days when demand hits certain thresholds.
 
 
    (5/3/2025) An interesting factoid has been posted about attendance at the Tokyo Disney Resort theme parks. According to the post, while attendance rose slightly in 2024 for the Oriental Land Company parks to 27.558k in 2024, compared to 27.507k in 2023, this was actually due only to a 20.7% increase in guests visiting the parks from overseas. Domestic tourism of the Japanese theme parks was actually down 2.8% in 2024.
    Projecting the future trends based on these numbers, they claim that domestic attendance will continue to decline by about 8% over the next decade, in which to stay even with current attendance levels the Tokyo Disney theme parks will need to lure in about 1.9 million international guests by 2035.

2027 - Sugar Rush: Sweet Rescue - (10/28/2026) The Disney Parks Blog has posted an update about the new Wreck-It Ralph themed attraction opening at Tokyo Disneyland in 2027. The new attraction’s official name is Sugar Rush: Sweet Rescue and we now have an opening date for it on April 15, 2027.
    King Candy is up to trouble once again in the Sugar Rush video game universe, and has transformed various innocent candies into his “Sugar Bugs” who have been reprogrammed “to consume the entire kingdom’. Guests will team up with Ralph, Vanellope and other Sugar Rush friends to try to restore the Sugar Bugs back to their original programming before they destroy the world of Sugar Rush forever. To do this guests will use built-in “scanners” installed in the ride vehicles which will keep track of your score.
    The Sugar Rush fun will also appear outside of the attraction with the opening of the new Pixel Prism gift shop next to the attraction on the same day, and two days later a new popcorn stand will open overlooking Tomorrowland Plaza that will serve a brand-new Sugar-Lime Popcorn flavor inspired by the Sugar Bugs. (As a new promotion, the new flavor will also be sold at select pop-corn stands throughout Tokyo Disneyland as well for a limited time).
    As part of the build-up promotion to the opening of the new attraction, a new Sugar Rush themed mini-game will be offered for download on mobile devices starting Jan. 12, 2027, and if you play it while in the park, a new special play area will be unlocked as well. This will be followed by a variety of Sugar Rush: Sweet Rescue inspired decorations and photo-ops will appear in Tomorrowland starting on Feb. 18, followed by themed projections added to the Cinderella Castle show starting on March 18.
    Of course, the big new addition coming to Tomorrowland, Space Mountain Earthrise, is also expected to open in late 2027 as well, so you can expect all the fun to continue in Tomorrowland all year long.

    (4/28/2024) Tokyo Disneyland surprised everyone with the announcement that the park would be closing down their current Buzz Lightyear’s Astro Blasters dark ride (that just turned 20 years old). The attraction will close in October 2024 and be replaced by a new interactive dark ride themed to the Wreck-It Ralph animated film series. According to the press release guests will “enter the candy-themed racing game Sugar Rush, which is being attacked by Sugar Bugs – glitches created by King Candy. Guests team up with the beloved film characters Ralph and Vanellope to transform the Sugar Bugs back to their original “kawaii” confectionery such as cookies and cakes.”
    While the final name for the new dark ride has yet to be announced,  the park did confirm that it would be ready to open sometime in 2026 and that the neighboring “Planet M” gift shop will also be rethemed.
    The timing is interesting, as the park’s Tomorrowland will be undergoing some significant changes over the next two years, with the closing of Space Mountain taking place on July 31st 2024, shortly before Buzz Lightyear closes. However the new version of Space Mountain will not be ready until sometime in 2027. The closure of both attractions, located right down the center of Tomorrowland itself, will create an interesting void, but also makes one wonder what other changes could be in store for Tokyo’s Tomorrowland going forward.
    This will also be the second of the Buzz Lightyear themed dark rides closed to be transformed into new attractions. The first one was at Hong Kong Disneyland which opened in 2005 and closed in 2017 to be transformed into a Marvel themed ‘Ant-Man and The Wasp: Nano Battle!” dark ride. Other than the soon to close ride in Tokyo, the other remaining Buzz Lightyear themed dark rides are located at Walt Disney World’s Magic Kingdom (opened 1998), Disneyland (2005), Disneyland Paris (2006) and Shanghai Disneyland (2016).

 

2027 - Space Mountain: Earthrise - (8/24/2026) We’ve got just about one more year or so until the new Space Mountain Earthrise is ready to open at Tokyo Disneyland in late 2027. So it’s time to talk a little bit more about what we know about this new attraction that is said to be the most expensive attraction ever built at the Tokyo Disney Resort, coming in at a budget of around 70.5+ billion yen, or $460 US million. That alone is amazing, as there are some small theme parks out there that were built for less than the cost of this one attraction.
    So what do we know so far? This will be a custom designed roller coaster system from Intamin, and despite what was seen earlier this year, the cars on the trains will absolutely not rotate like the ones from Guardians of the Galaxy in Epcot. The long rumored tilting launch track that will position the coaster onto the inclined launch track is going to happen, and for the first time ever this will be a Space Mountain powered by electromagnetic launches. Yes… that was no mistake, as I did indeed use the plural form there, as due to the layout and length of the track inside, the trains on Space Mountain Earthrise will pass through several magnetic booster tracks along the way to ensure that the ride maintains a top speed throughout the entire layout. I’m sure some of these booster elements will be themed to become part of the overall experience, while some could be hidden.
    The fun factoid is that if the rise ever e-stops, the trains will actually be able to use these booster track zones to get the trains back up to speed and moving again.     (1/3/2026) The early word is that Tokyo Disneyland’s new “Space Mountain” might be getting a slight name-change when it opens, to differentiate it from the old version. Nothing has been officially mentioned by the Oriental Land Company at this time, but the name “Space Mountain Earthrise” is rumored to be under consideration.
    With the new attraction not set to open until sometime in 2027, there is still a lot of time to work on the name, and Disney has an interesting history of name changes when it comes to the “Space Mountain” brand at international parks. While Disneyland Paris opened in 1992 without a Space Mountain at all, they quickly announced that one would be ready to open by 1995, but it would be named “Discovery Mountain” instead. By the time the attraction opened, they had changed their mind and renamed it Space Mountain again to take better advantage of the brand recognition, but also expanded that name to be “Space Mountain - De la Terre a la lune” for the first decade, then after a remodel they changed it to “Space Mountain: Mission 2” from 2005 to 2017. Ironically the Space Mountain name has now been dropped entirely in favor of a Star Wars theme being applied to the ride experience, and it is now known as “Hyperspace Mountain”.

    (4/28/22)  Tokyo Disneyland surprised everyone with the announcement that the park’s Space Mountain attraction will be undergoing a complete overhaul. As part of the announcement, new concept art was released that definitely gives the iconic mountain attraction an all new look that will set it apart from its American counterparts.
    Gone is the old classic hard-line design and find a new Space Mountain design that seems to have taken on more of a swooping organic style of design instead. According to the release this will be an “entirely new attraction” featuring new “immersive special effects”, but will maintain the original concept of an indoor roller coaster ride through outer space.
    As you may have guessed, this seems to be a complete rebuild of the attraction that will see the original Tokyo Disneyland Space Mountain close for good sometime in 2024 and reopen sometime in 2027. The project will also see a new enhanced plaza built around the new Space Mountain that will “express the connection between Earth and the universe, representing an image of a future where humans are in harmony with nature.””
    No further details were given about what the new attraction experience may offer but I am very curious. Tokyo’s Space Mountain opened in 1983 and was built essentially as a clone of the Disneyland version that opened in 1977 and later closed for it’s own rebuild in 2003. When the Disneyland version was rebuilt over a two years period, they really only rebuilt the actual roller coaster ride inside of the structure. It isn’t known if any of Tokyo’s original structure will remain, or if this will be a complete rebuild.

 

2029 - Tokyo Disney Cruise Line - (2/9/2025) While the Oriental Land Company (OLC) has already signed an agreement with Disney to for the license and creation of their very own Disney branded cruise ship that will be a copy of one of Disney’s current ship designs, the vessel will not be run by the Disney Cruise Line itself.     (1/12/2025) Oriental Land Company announced a future plan to expand their operations beyond just the Tokyo Disney Resort, but to move into the cruise line business. According to this article, as they find that they no longer have the available property to expand their theme park operations, they are looking to add a cruise line as a way to expand their offerings into the future.
    The plan would be to launch sometime during fiscal 2028, starting operations with a single very-large ship able to accommodate around 4,000 passengers, that would serve as an “offshore theme park” style experience, blending the magic of the Tokyo Disney Resort with the expectations of a luxury cruise line experience through a variety of 2 to 4 night itineraries.
    In mid-2024 they did sign an agreement to have the Meyer Werft shipyard to construction a Disney Wish class style ship for them. The ship will be Disney branded, but will be owned and operated by the Oriental Land Company, who will keep the ship’s operations based entirely in Japan, with the first cruise expected to be launched with guests sometime in 2029.
