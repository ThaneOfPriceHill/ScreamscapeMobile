    (1/4/2026) The Jurassic Park River Adventure attraction at Islands of Adventure is now officially closed, and will remain closed for a major refurbishment to the attraction that will keep it down until November 2026.
    We’ve reported that this was coming for some time now, but as you may also recall, our sources always confirmed that this planned refurbishment would not see the attraction changed from Jurassic Park to Jurassic World. Instead of following in the park of what happened at the Hollywood park, it sounds like the Orlando refurbishment will be more like the one that took place in Japan. In that case the Jurassic Park ride at Universal Studios Japan was closed from September 2023 until January 2025, so even longer than the current Orlando project.
    In Japan and attraction also kept it’s original Jurassic Park theme, and focused on updating and refreshing the attraction’s extensive list of effects sequences over the 16 month closure, updating the look and technology all around to create a better “show” for the guests.
    Keeping in mind just how much damage water rides suffer from year after year just from the wet environment. Also remember that the Orlando ride opened in 1999, two years before the Japanese version opened in 2001. According to some social media rumors, the budget for this 11-month long refurbishment of Orlando’s Jurassic Park is thought to be around $14.8 million. Of course, I’m sure a big part of this may go towards updated dinosaur animatronics, and I wouldn’t be surprised if the attraction also updated the flume drop and possibly purchased a new fleet of boats as well, and that isn’t cheap by any means. Just like John Hammond says, they spared no expense.
    So in the meantime, we’ll just have to wait and see what happens as we get closure to the ride reopening. However, with much of the attraction layout hidden from view, I don’t think the internet spies will have too much to report on unless they spot new dinosaurs being lowered into position.
 
    (9/17/2025) Screamscape has previously mentioned that the Jurassic Park River Adventure was preparing to close for some kind of extended refurbishment this winter, and now we finally have confirmation from Universal.
    The updated refurbishment schedule now lists that the Jurassic Park River Adventure will close down starting January 5, 2026… and remain closed for practically the rest of 2026, with a tentative reopening date of November 19, 2026.
 
    (3/29/2025) Rumor has it that we can expect to see the Jurassic Park River Adventure at Islands of Adventure go down for what may be a longer than normal winter refurbishment towards the end of 2025. Now before the rumor mill kicks into gear thinking that this could be a conversion to add a Jurassic World themed makeover like what happened with the Hollywood version of the ride, I’m told that this is not the case at all. There are still said to be no plans to make the Orlando ride anything like the Hollywood version.

    (12/20/2025) WDWNT has posted that Universal Orlando has filed construction paperwork with the county for a major new attraction that seems set to come to what is currently the remains of the Lost Continent section of the park. It isn’t known for sure exactly what section this will take over, as it could be for the former Poseidon’s Fury area, the former Eighth Voyage of Sindbad show area, or pretty much everything in between. The one telling part of the filing however is that the contractor for the project is Intamin.
     While this typically has meant that the new attraction is a roller coaster (as Intamin was behind Hagrid and the Velocicoaster and is currently installing the new Fast & Furious Hollywood Drift spinning coaster at the California park) the company is also behind a number of other unique non-coaster ride concepts built at parks across the world. Some major innovations as of late that have received a lot of praise include the new Dynamic Motion Stage used for Danse Macabe at Efteling last year as well as the Dome Ride Theater used for Hypersphere 360 at SeaWorld Abu Dhabi. Intamin is also well known for the water rides and drop towers, though Islands of Adventure isn’t likely needing either of those. They did introduce a new ride concept with ETF that would combine trackless dark ride vehicle technology that could enter onto a section of magnetic launch track called the Multi-Dimension Mover.
    A previous permit filing posted elsewhere also indicated that the while the construction start date was December 2025, the completion date is also listed as December 2027, so whatever new attraction is coming to Islands of Adventure will likely be planned to open by Summer 2027.

 
    (9/13/2025) In a quick update, Universal Orlando has apparently confirmed their plans to begin demolition of what’s left of much of the Lost Continent section of Islands of Adventure in preparation of whatever new plans they have in place for the site. Mythos will be staying of course, but it seems they have confirmed that the building structures for the former Eighth Voyage of Sindbad show and the Poseidon’s Fury attraction will be coming down.
    While theme parks are always subject to change over the years as new attractions replace the old, and new themed are painted over faded ones, this could mark the first time that one of the actual “lands”, or in this case an entire themed “island” at the park would be entirely wiped out, and replaced with something brand new.
    Look for demolition work to begin sometime in the next month or two, though it technically could start in backstage areas unseen before it gets noticed within the park itself. So what will replace Lost Continent? Well… there hasn’t been anything NEW rumored in awhile, but I can confirm that I’ve heard of at least two different Legend of Zelda concepts over the years. One of which was going to keep the Poseidon’s Fury concept of being a walk-through attraction / live show concept, while the other involved a new ride concept that would start out as a flume ride and end up transforming your vehicle into a flying craft held aloft by hidden tracks in the roof as you flew across the world of Hyrule to battle Ganon in an epic showdown before being placed back into the water.
    Now we’ll have to wait and see if this will be a new Nintendo concept, or something perhaps entirely new. I do have to mention one small technicality with any new Nintendo concept that could block this from coming to IOA so soon. I can’t verify exactly how true this is, but someone once told me that Nintendo’s Shigeru Miyamoto commented once to Japanese media that as part of the deal with Universal that the new Nintendo themed attractions had to open in the Japanese theme park first. So obviously, in terms of Super Nintendo World, this has held true, with even the Donkey Kong coaster opening in Japan a few months ahead of the preview rides starting on the Orlando version. I’ve been unable to find any record of this comment myself just yet, and things are always subject to translation errors, but it is possible that this comment was simply meant for the first wave of announced Super Nintendo World attractions and not future projects, or in relations to non-core (Mario-verse) Nintendo character projects like Metroid or Zelda. If anyone has been able to find that quote from Miyamoto-san, please do forward me a link.

    (4/12/2023) After being closed for a long period of time during COVID, only to reopen after a lengthy rehab as well… Universal Orlando shocked many today when they announced via an internal employee email that the Poseidon’s Fury attraction would be closing for good at the end of May 9th, 2023 to make room for something new at Islands of Adventure.
    So what is on the horizon? No one knows for sure, but when The Eighth Voyage of Sindbad stunt show closed in 2018, I figured that Poseidon’s Fury was likely to be living on borrowed time. It’s also fair to assume that The Lost Contient island in general will also be lost to the sands of time, as after May 10th, both of the remaining attractions that made up the themed zone will be no more, leaving just a random assortment of gift shops, food stands and a foul tempered fountain.  Oh, and Mythos… the park’s premier dining restaurant.
    It would be interesting if Mythos was also shut down, but so far nothing has been said about that. Even if Mythos stays, the combined land used by the closed Sindbad and Poseideon attractions, along with the connecting shops is almost comparable in size to the land used for the Hagrid’s Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure attraction. I’m not saying this will happen yet, but if Universal was willing to do so, they could level this entire space to the ground and be able to repurpose it for just about anything… a new major attraction to a whole land with several smaller attractions.
    Universal seems to have something in mind however, as the email mentioned sharing more details about what might replace Poseidon’s Fury in the future. So it is also entirely possible that they could just reskin the existing Poseidon’s Fury attraction to create another similar walk-through adventure. I know at one point, many years ago, a concept was pitched to turn Posideon into a Legend of Zelda attraction, but those plans were never approved. Plus, from what I understand, the basic layout and effects gags from Poseidon would have remained intact, including the water vortex, the only change would be the story and theme of everything, so it makes sense why Universal never went through with it.

 

???? - The Hobbit / The Lord of the Rings - Rumor - (9/6/2026) Is The Lord of the Rings coming to life as a theme park world? The creation of a theme park land theme to the J.R.R. Tolkien’s land of Middle Earth and themed to either The Hobbit or Lord of the Rings films has been a hotly discussed topic for decades now. Much of the speculation regarding the possibility of such a project has been tied to Universal Orlando.
    While Universal has never announced such a project, they have name dropped Tolkien’s works many times over the years in guest surveys along with countless other IPs to gauge guest reactions to determine what they should maybe look into building in the future. In social media circles, there has been a relentless push from fans who desire to see Universal bring this world to life in one form or another. In fact, many were pushing hard to let Universal know that they wanted to see Middle-Earth built as one of the world’s in Epic Universe, but it was not to be.
    So when Middle-Earth Enterprises filed a new trademark application in late August to reserve the name, “The Shire” in both English and Spanish, to be used for “theme park services”, theme park fans were quick to take notice. The now total loss of The Lost Continent area at the Islands of Adventure theme park for an unknown new project is certainly foremost on the minds of fans here, as Universal has yet to announce their plans here yet. 
    Islands of Adventure has long been regarded as the most likely home for any kind of Lord of the Rings development, as the literary history of the IP ties in the best to the original central theme of Islands of Adventure, which was built with the idea that every “island” in the park had ties to IPs that were originally works on the printed page, from comic books, newspaper funnies, kids books to full novels. To this end, another Island in the park was long being looked at as to bring Middle-Earth to life as a re-theme and potential replacement for Toon Lagoon. The old newspaper funnies theme of Toon Lagoon no longer really fits with current guest demographics, and yet rides like Popeye & Bluto’s Bilge-Rat Barges are still very popular. So one long-standing theory was that if The Hobbit were to take over Toon Lagoon, Popeye’s rapids could possible get rethemed to tie into the River Barrel Escape scene from The Desolation of Smaug film and allow the rest of the land to be rebuilt entirely.
    While this is still all speculation, it is interesting to see Middle-Earth Enterprises file a US trademark for something specifically for use at a theme park. But when it comes to protecting your trademark, a lot of the legal issues arise over if the owner of an IP took appropriate steps to not only file for their trademarks, but to take the necessary work to protect them as well from any possible infringement. So this could just be Middle-Earth Enterprises working to cover all their bases.
