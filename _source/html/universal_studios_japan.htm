    (9/1/2026) According to the Japan Times, Universal Studios Japan is said to have requested to begin leasing the land to the north of the theme park at the conclusion of the current lease agreement in 2028. The goal would be to use this property to expand the park and add even more attractions, to allow for the ongoing growth of the Universal Studios Japan theme park.
 
    (8/29/2026) It has been reported that a major expansion announcement is expected to be coming soon from Universal Studios Japan, at a budget estimated to be between 200-300 billion yen ($1.25 to $1.87 billion US dollars). The expansion is also expected to make use of about 60,000 square meters of nearby land (nearly 15 acres) outside the park currently owned by the city of Osaka that was formerly used for a Nippon Steel factory to the North of the park.

   Of note as the details about one particular horror attraction… Afterimage. It isn’t listed on the english version of the website site, but the Japanese site describes it as actually having an age restriction that won’t permit anyone under 17 to see it, and it will require an extra cost ticket (1,900 yen) as well as the signing of a waiver.

    (6/24/2026) Universal Studios Japan has launched an interesting new piece of wearable tech to help visually impaired guests navigate through the park. According to Universal Destinations & Experiences, this is the first time that this particular piece of technology has been put to use within a theme park.
    The item in question is a small wearble device, not unlike an old-school pager, that is attached to the guests shoe. The device connects to an app downloaded onto the guest’s smartphone and then provides physical “direction cues through foot vibrations, rather than audio or screens.” Testing began last month with guests who had guide dogs now rentable at the park for 1,000 yen ($6) for the day.

 
    (5/10/2026) Universal Studios Japan has announced a new nighttime festival for the park this summer called “Universal Summer Festival Night - Neon Glow Up”. This will take place from July 1st to August 26, 2026, and feature a number of light displays, interactive shows, dance performances, as well as special food and merchandise items. One of the highlights will be the “NO LIMIT! Glow Up” show taking place in the Gramercy Park area that will feature a number of park characters, including those from Sesame Street and the Minions.
    It is also worth mentioning that today was the last day for Sesame Street Fan World, which started out in the park as one of the opening day attraction areas.

2027/2028 - Pokemon Land / Detective Pikachu Dark Ride - Rumor - (1/25/2026) While we still don’t know exactly what is coming, Universal Studios Japan has made an official announcement confirming (or rather re-confirming...) that they have signed a deal with “The Pokemon Company” to bring a “New Pokemon Experience” to the park. This will be “Based on a hyper-realistic experience that stimulates all five senses, it brings the Pokémon world to life in an unprecedentedly interactive and rich way. Currently preparing for its world premier at Universal Studios Japan. …Evolving!”
   So what exactly does this mean? It’s worth pointing out that at no point was this mentioned as a “Universal Cool Japan” themed temporary attraction. This appears to be something big planned not only for Universal Studios Japan… but the way it was phrased having a “world premier” at Universal Studios Japan sort of mimics the way that Super Nintendo World was first announced back in 2015, as a new land and attractions that would first premier at the Japanese park before being allowed to open at Universal’s US parks. This was also confirmed by Universal's own press release about bringing the world of Pokemon to life “in new and enriching ways for the first time. Following its debut in Japan, Universal Destinations & Experiences will deliver unique Pokemon experiences across the company’s global footprint.”
    This should come as no surprise, as Nintendo is involved once again after all in this project, as they are listed as a 1/3rd owner of The Pokemon Company. This may come as a surprise to some as many have long assumed that Pokemon was just another IP owned by Nintendo. While Nintendo does dictate a lot of control over the IP, the company is owned by three Japanese corporate creators: Game Freak (the video game developer), Creatures (a game developer who was also behind the trading card game) and Nintendo who acts as the publisher, and global IP owner outside of Japan. Oh… and if you remember, this is not the first announcement of a deal between Pokemon and Universal, who first announced they were going to begin working together back in 2021 on something that was expected to begin in 2022. Clearly this was delayed, like in response to the delayed opening of Japan’s Super Nintendo World from 2020 to Spring 2021 and then Donkey Kong Country did not open until December 2024.
    With that project now out of the way… and Universal not quite ready to announce exactly what they are up to… it does seem clear that we will likely see a whole Pokemon themed area come to the park, that would have to be anchored by a major new attraction. As I’ve been posting since that first 2021 announcement, I believe we should be focusing on the closed Spider-Man attraction area for this new project. As predicted, Spider-Man closed forever in Japan at the end of Jan. 22, 2024. Clearly it was no coincidence that the updated Pokemon announcement from Universal was tweeted out on the exact same day, 2 years later, on Jan. 22, 2026. And going back to the start of our rumors about the fate of Spider-Man, it was felt that crews were working on a Pokemon or Detective Pikachu themed replacement for the Spider-Man attraction, one that might even use an updated version of the old ride system. My best guess is that something along these lines is still in the works, something that could open in mid to late 2027 or 2028. With that in mind, I do have to believe that the “interactive” nature of a Pokemon land could also be planning to create a Pokeball style gadget that the park can sell to guests to wander around the land with, using interactive stations not unlike they created for the interactive Wands used in the Wizarding World, in which guests can try to catch their own Pokemon.
    Currently the closed Spider-Man attraction resides within the New York section of the park, which lies next to the old San Francisco section of the park which has been heavily under construction over the past few years, being transformed into the new Minion Land area where the park’s former Back To The Future and Backdraft attractions were transformed into Despicable Me: Minion Mayhem and the new Villain-Con Minion Blast Attractions. Gazing over at the New York area next door, we already saw Spider-Man closed for good in 2024, and the park’s Terminator 2-3D attraction that was also located in the New York area closed was first closed in late 2020 under “COVID” related guidelines when the park reopened. However it wasn’t until 2023 when the park confirmed that T2-3D was gone for good. Following that the unique 4D theater attraction space has thus far only been by the temporary Detective Conan 4-D show that opened in Spring 2024. The land itself is laid out in very similar fashion to the New York area of Universal Studios Florida, where Spider-Man inhabited the building where Florida’s Revenge of the Mummy now sits, and Terminator-2 (now Detective Conan) sitting in the space where Twister (now Race Through New York) sits, with the open Gramercy Park themed space and small themed building surrounding that, creating an open plaza area that could be reformatted into a number of interesting possibilities as a new Pokemon themed area. Move a little further down the pathway towards the park entrance, and next door you will also find the Space Fantasy indoor coaster attraction  that replaced the park’s original ET ride. However, Space Fantasy is rarely ever run as it was designed, as the park routinely uses the attraction with VR headgear and many different themes over the years as part of their annual Universal Cool Japan events, making it also possibly ripe for a Pokemon themed redevelopment.
    If I had to guess, we’ll find out more about what’s going on here later this year, but in the meantime, if you visit the park, keep your eyes out for clues here.

    (1/17/2024) It’s hard to believe it, but in just a few short days the Adventures of Spider-Man will close forever at Universal Studios Japan. While the park has yet to announce what will replace it, the adventures of your friendly neighborhood Spider-Man will all come to a sudden end on Monday, January 22, 2024.
 
    (5/17/2023) Universal Studios Japan has confirmed that The Adventures of Spider-Man ride will close forever at the end of January 22, 2024. As the attraction first opened in January 2004, it sounds like Universal is running it though to the very end of their current IP licensing agreement. Now before you argue and say that Universal has a never-ending agreement to use the Marvel characters, that agreement is only for Universal’s use of the characters in the eastern half of North America, and was really only used in the Islands of Adventure theme park in Florida. The agreement to use the Spider-Man IP for the attraction in Japan had an entirely separate contract, and only for the IPs used in the Spider-Man attraction itself.    
    While nothing was said about what might replace it, the rumor we reported back in 2021 still holds water… that Spider-Man was expected to close down sometime in 2024 in order to make way for some kind of new ride with a Pokemon or Pikachu theme about 18 to 24 months later.

    (10/8/21) According to press release, Universal Studios Japan has officially entered into a long-term partnership with The Pokemon Company “that will immerse guests into the world of Pokemon with with innovative technology and creativity beginning in 2022.“
    So what exactly does this mean? If you recall, back in May we posted a report that the park was likely preparing to retire their popular Adventures of Spider-Man dark ride, and if the rumors hold true, it would evolve into a new Pokemon themed attraction. While 2022 is to fast of the timeline to transform the dark ride into something Pokemon themed, especially since it hasn’t even closed yet, I suspect we’ll see Pokemon added to the park in some other new way as a slow build up to the eventual announcement of the Pokemon themed dark ride project. Given how rapidly the park has been able to add various highly themed temporary attractions over the years as part of the Universal Cool Japan series of events, I wouldn’t be surprised to see some of this magic put in place throughout 2022 to add several temporary Pokemon attraction, possibly including an VR overlay on the park’s Space Fantasy indoor coaster.
