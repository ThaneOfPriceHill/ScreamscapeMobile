    (9/4/2026) I hate to say this… I really do… but my gut feeling right now is that if you never had the chance to ride X or X2 over the past 24 years… you probably aren’t going to get that opportunity. While we are still waiting for Six Flags to make some kind of official announcement, my gut feeling says, “stick a fork in her, she’s done”. Meanwhile various guests to the park across social media have been chiming in to report that they also felt that the ride experience on X2 has gotten significantly rougher over the years than what it used to be.
    While the official end may simply be coming due to the mounting number of injury cases, there have always been some other factors that have made be wonder about the long-term viability of the original Arrow Dynamics 4th Dimension coaster (now S&S Worldwide) as a design from a domestic and international viewpoint.
    Going back to the origin of the 4th Dimension concept, Arrow Dynamics was never looking to build something as large and as aggressive as X originally. Looking back at archives from the old Arrow Dynamics website, they actually first pitched four different layouts of the 4th Dimension concept to start with, with cute names like Mayhem, Delirium, Quantum and Octophobia. It is noteworthy that all four designs only stood between 92 and 127 feet in height, and none featured the infamous 210 foot vertical helldive that X is known for. Clearly Arrow was looking to start out this new design with a more mild experience, and then be able to work up to something bigger after they had time to perfect the concept in the real world.
    What happened, as told to me first hand back in the day within the halls of the S&S HQ in Logan, Utah was that things took an interesting turn when Gary Story, the CEO of Six Flags from the 90’s through to 2003, was first pitched the idea for X by Arrow Dynamics. While I don’t know the exact timeline, I assume this took place somewhere between 1998 and 2000, when the biggest new development in roller coaster technology had just arrived on the scene in Atlon Towers with the opening of Oblivion in 1998… B&M’s first Dive Machine roller coaster. Clearly the arrival of Oblivion was weighing on Gary Story’s mind, because when presented with the more mild early 4th Dimension designs, he was said to have asked if they could built one 200 feet tall with a vertical drop. Not wanting to disappoint, the Arrow sales team apparently said that they COULD do it, but that they would rather build a smaller one first. According to the tale, Mr. Story then told Arrow he was only interested if they could build one with at least a 200 foot vertical dive drop. And the rest of history, as deals were signed and the design team went about the creation of the ride that would become world famous as “X” that opened in 2002 at Six Flags Magic Mountain.
    What has always bothered me over the years was that while Six Flags seemed to have achieved global recognition of the unique X / 4th Dimension ride system, they never did try to replicate this success by ordering any other further roller coasters over the years that followed. Coming from the same theme park chain that built a Batman: The Ride inverted roller coaster clone from B&M almost every year for over a decade certainly seems to say “something” about how they felt about X, without ever saying anything. Along similar lines… once S&S bought out Arrow and later came up with 4D Free Spin coaster design that offered similar flip-seat style thrills as X but at a much smaller scale, Six Flags did jump on that bandwagon and opened seven of them between 2015 and 2019 across the chain.
    Only two other 4th Dimension coasters were ever built from S&S, Eejanaika at Fuji-Q in Japan in 2006 and later Dinoconda opened at China’s Dinosaur Park in 2012. The design of these two are very similar to the original design from X, just with some slight modifications to improve the design. Right now it is Eejanaika at Fuji-Q I would be keeping a very close eye on, as during the COVID pandemic Fuji-Q underwent a similar issue of guest injury complaints about another S&S made roller coaster… Do-Dononpa. This was S&S’s highly aggressive “Thrust Air” launched coaster that had already undergone a track upgrade to swap out the Top Hat hill with a giant vertical loop in 2017. Following that the coaster only ran up through 2021 when the park closed it down to investigate why they were seeing a sudden ride in guest injury complaints. From December 2020 to August 2021 when the coaster was shut down, Fuji-Q was said to have reported 18 guest injuries ranging from bone fractures, cervical spine compression fractures, and neck and chest fractures. Of the 18 injuries, up to 9 of them reported suffering from broken bones, and after sitting closed for several years, Fuji-Q announced they were closing and removing Do-Dononpa for good.
    Now this is an entirely different roller coaster, using an entirely different train and restraint system, so I’m not saying that the injuries are in any way connected. What I am saying is that we should keep an eye on Fuji-Q to see if they react in any way with their operations on Eejanaika in response to the reported issues on X2 at Six Flags Magic Mountain. Since Fuji-Q already went through a similar problem of guest injuries reported after riding a roller coaster at their park, I’m sure they will be extra cautious about any issues reported from Eejanaika.
    So this is all just something to think about as we wait to find out the fate of X2.

2027- Thrill Glider - (9/10/2026) Six Flags Magic Mountain has announced their new roller coaster for 2027 at last… as… THRILL GLIDER. Yeah, I know I was hoping for a something a little more customized in the name department, but you know what? I’m going to allow it because if the actual coaster looks remotely like how they show it off in the artwork and animation with all the little custom theming bits here and there, then I’m sold, because this is a fantastic looking roller coaster that will fit in right at Magic Mountain. As previously predicted, the Vekoma Thrill Glider is essentially their motorbike style roller coaster seating configuration, mounted to an inverted roller coaster track system. Themed as hovercycles, riders will assume a prone, face-forward position suspended beneath the track.
Thrill Glider is going to claim several bullet-point items:
    World’s First Multi-Launch Suspended ThrillGlider
    World’s Fastest ThrillGlider
    World’s Longest ThrillGlider
    World’s Tallest ThrillGlider
    Most Inversions on a ThrillGlider
And hey… for about a year this will be the world’s ONLY ThrillGlider, though there are currently 2 more confirmed to be on the way. (Race coming to Futuroscope in 2028 and Missie Luminar to Efteling in 2029).
    So what are the hard numbers for Thrill Glider?
2 launches, up to 50 mph top speed, standing 107 feet tall and it will blast you through 5 inversions along 3380 feet of track, over 12 airtime moments, over the thrilling 80-second adventure. There will be 2 trains available, each able to hold 16 riders, with a minimum height requirement of 48”, so a perfect thrill for those who may not yet be tall enough to tackle the big B&M coasters just yet.
    There is even a little storyline to go with the coaster, about how a “QuantumPulse Propulsion Systems” company is using the coaster to highlight a powerful new energy source. “The journey begins long before takeoff in "The Futurist Pavilion," a sleek showcase facility filled with dynamic displays, holographic presentations and scientific exhibits detailing the company's discovery of PSR B417, a powerful new energy source with the potential to transform tomorrow. As riders make their way through the queue, they catch glimpses of towering hexagonal collectors embedded into the hillside, harvesting energy from distant pulsars in space. Upon entering the load station, illuminated blue energy lines, aerodynamic architecture and pulsing atmospheric audio build anticipation as riders prepare to board QuantumPulse's latest breakthrough. Once launched, the power of PSR B417 will be felt firsthand as the vehicles soar above the landscape through sweeping maneuvers, sudden dives and weightless moments that simulate piloting a high-speed hovercycle powered by tomorrow's technology. Blending engaging storytelling, futuristic design and advanced coaster technology, Thrill Glider delivers a one-of-a-kind adventure that transports guests into an optimistic vision of the future.”
    So if all the little tunnel theming and hex style portals light up at night, this is going to be a major attraction that guests are going to want to ride at night as well as in the daytime. Plus the little hex portals do remind me of my favorite roller coaster right in my own backyard… FURY 325 at Carowinds.

Keep in mind that these are just wild rumors at this point, with  nothing close to being verified… however… I’d be remiss if I didn’t admit that I had heard most of these rumors before in one form or another over the past few years.
    The control and direction of the Six Flags chain has been itself under a wild ride over the past decade, falling under the guidance of several different leaders, each with their own vision of how they wanted to do things, as well as different priorities of which parks would be more in focus than others. Now with the latest political shift complete, finalizing the merger of Six Flags and Cedar Fair, it isn’t strange at all that some of the older rumors may be returning to light as the new leadership calling the shots is more known for their advanced planning efforts than the previous ones.
    So with that in mind, I’m hearing that the focus of the park, following the opening of the 2026 roller coaster, will be to tackle a redesign of the park’s Samurai Summit district, which essentially would affect all the rides at the top of the mountain itself. The good news is that I’ve heard nothing at all about making any changes to Tatsu, but it is sounding like everything else up there would be under review.
    This includes the closed Superman: The Escape (aka: Escape From Krypton) and I’m hearing that it may be very unlikely that Superman would ever reopen again. The one thing Superman has going for it is that the park does not want to lose the Lex Luthor: Drop of Doom ride attached to the sides of the Superman tower. Currently Lex Luthor is now the world record holder for being the tallest vertical drop tower ride in the world, offering a dizzying 415 foot drop to the ground below.  While Lex Luthor’s height was surpassed by Zumanjaro at Six Flags Great Adventure, the demolition of the Kingda Ka tower that it was attached to has returned the record to Magic Mountain, or at least until the Sirocco Tower at Six Flags Qiddiya opens. I’ve been hearing that the difference in the tower structures and how these individual rides were built means that it is possible for the tower itself and Lex Luthor to stay in place, while still removing the launch tracks and station for the Superman coaster.
    In a previous announcement, but under the old management, it was confirmed that the park was looking to reopen the closed Skytower at the top of the mountain, so hopefully this will still fall into the efforts of the future Samurai Summit renovation plans, along with renovations to other existing facilities on the mountain, and is also rumored to include the creation of an all new thrill ride.
    There are rumors of another loss for Samurai Summit however, with an increasing number of whispers claiming that Ninja’s lifespan may be coming to an end. The coaster which opened in 1988 was the West Coast’s only Arrow Suspended coaster and was extremely popular for a long time, featuring a winding layout hidden by trees along the mountainside. Over the years most of those trees were removed (especially after one fell on the ride) and Ninja also suffered a little damage over the years from various Earthquakes, but it still managed to survive all these years relatively intact. If the rumors are to be believed, the long term planning efforts of the park to build the rumored Vekoma “thrill glider” coaster in 2026 is thought to be meant as a replacement for Ninja. If so, 2025 could be your last year to experience Ninja… or they might be able to keep it around for 2026 as well. Unlike how we’ve seen this year’s crop of closed rides just closed and demolished without warning, the early word is that the park is planning to give fans a healthy advanced warning as to when they plan to close Ninja for good.
    Again… all rumors… but if you visit the park, do yourself a favor and get a few good rides in on Ninja while you still can. Nothing lasts forever…
