    (7/3/2026) A bit of a disturbance took place at Its A Small World at Walt Disney World this week when a guest’s portable phone charger unit caught fire while they were waiting in the queue for the attraction. A Disney CM lept to action with a fire extinguisher to put out the flaming battery unit and no injuries were reported, though the attraction and queue were evacuated of course for a time to ensure everything was ok.
    Portable battery units (aka: Power Banks) than can be used to recharge phones, cameras and other small portable electronics without the need to plug into a wall socket have become an essential travel accessory to many over the years. The devices have also been increasingly in news reports over the past few years when they’ve suddenly burst into flames like this, not only in public spaces but even on board airplanes who now require that the devices be kept within arms reach at all times. But how does this happen?
    According to a quick google search, the power units ignite when they enter a phase called “thermal runaway”, which can be triggered by a short circuit or physical damage that cause the unit to overheat internally beyond specifications. As the heat increases, it speeds up chemical reactions inside the battery itself that begin to generate even more heat until it reaches the point where the liquid electrolyte inside catches fire.
    Safety guides recommend not buying cheap knock-off or “counterfeit” style battery units, as they may lack proper insulation and overload protection. If you unit appears to have suffered physical damage, you would be better off replacing it with a new device, as damage can easily tear the thin films inside the battery and making it easier for it to short circuit. And an important tip for anyone visiting theme parks…don’t leave your power bank in direct sunlight or anywhere it may come into contact with an extremely hot surface… and especially don’t leave them sitting in a hot car! And finally… don’t leave your devices plugged into a batter unit for an excessive amount of time just to keep it in a charged state. In the end, if anything about your battery unit looks damaged, swollen, if it gets strangely hot while being used… you might want to retire it from use.

    (5/28/2026) Walt Disney World dropped a surprise announcement earlier today when they confirmed that a complete update was coming to the Carousel of Progress. While the Carousel of Progress has started out 60 years ago as a project that Walt Disney himself helped create originally for the 1964-1965 New York World’s Fair. Following the fair the attraction featuring an animatronic family revealing the details of their lives through the decades as technology changed the world was moved to California and set up inside Disneyland in 1967 (with a new finale) before being moved once again to Walt DIsney World in 1975.
    Over the years, while the attraction has occasionally seen some small updates here and there (the last one was in 1994) the attraction has still largely remained the same. Now, according to Disney, the “Carousel of Progress was designed to celebrate change, not to stand still. As Imagineers, we now have the great responsibility of keeping Walt’s ideas of innovation and forward-looking spirate alive as we continue building the attraction’s future together.”
    So some all new updates and an entirely new scene are being added, with the new scene itself to feature an animatronic Walt Disney inspired by the 1964-special, “Disneyland Goes to the World’s Fair” where Walt first introduced the idea of the Carousel of Progress attraction.
    After this new opening scene, there will be updates given to all of the scenes, moving them forward into all new time periods “where we’ll get to see what John, Sarah, and the rest of the family are up to as they navigate ever-changing technology in new decades.” Of course, you’ll still get to hear the classic song, “There’s a Great Big Beautiful Tomorrow” play as the Carousel moves between scenes.
    The new acts will include:
Act 1 - The 1960’s
Act 2 - The 1980’s
Act 3 - The New Millennium
And Act 4 - The Possible Future
    The current version of Carousel of Progress will be closed starting on July 6th, and it is now expected to reopen until sometime in 2027.

2029/2030 - NEW Space Mountain - Rumor - (7/11/2026) Many of the Disney blogger sites are starting to report that a possible big announcement at D23 in August may be a plan to rebuild Space Mountain at Walt Disney World. The idea would be to close, gut and completely re-engineer the the attraction as a completely brand new roller coaster experience, not unlike what is happening right now at Tokyo Disneyland. Unlike in Japan however, the rumors suggest that the existing structure for the attraction would be kept and a new ride (with one track instead of two) would be designed and placed inside, to allow the existing infrastructure, underground queue and exit pathways all to remain in place.
    Longtime readers may also recall that going as far back of 2020 or 2021, when Tron: Lightcycle Run construction had begun, Screamscape had begun to hear this very same rumor. Long before Japan announced their project… the rumors we had posted claimed that Disney knew very well that Space Mountain was on it’s last legs and needed to be replaced, and a major reason that Tron was approved and built where it was, is that it would allow for Tomorrowland to still have a major E-Ticket roller coaster attraction so that the Magic Kingdom could close Space Mountain and re-imagine it with modern design and technology as the last renovations done to it in 2009 were simply just a band-aid to get another 15-20 years or so out of it.
    As the history books will show, the opening of Tron was delayed until 2023… and by then Disney had racked up a few other big projects they had to tackle first, so Space Mountian missed the originally planned window. Splash Mountain closed in early 2023 to undergo major renovations and reopen as Tiana’s Bayou Adventure in 2024, and then it was Big Thunder Mountain’s turn to close in early 2025 for a nearly 18 month refurbishment as well. As you can see, Disney has been on a bit of a refurbishment binge on their big attractions, not just at the Magic Kingdom, but many of the Kingdom parks across the world have also been serving up extended refurb schedules for some of the big classic attractions. If I’m not mistaken, at one point in 2025 I think the Big Thunder Mountain coasters in Paris, California and Florida were all closed for extended rebuilds at the same time.
    So now the Magic Kingdom has just closed Carousel of Progress until sometime in 2027, and from the sound of the latest rumors… Space Mountain may very well finally get its turn to go under the knife. The question is… will Disney take the opportunity to perhaps borrow some design concepts from the new one under construction in Japan right now, or will they create something perhaps more similar but more classic in style, like the Disneyland version that was also gutted and rebuilt between 2003 and 2005.
    So then the question becomes… where will this Space Mountain rebuild some within the greater time-table of projects happening at the Magic Kingdom right now? We’ve got Carousel of Progress opening in 2027… work has begun on the Cars: Piston Peak area with an unknown opening timeline and beyond that we’ve also got the Villains Land project. If I had to guess, and they are ready to close Space Mountain down by 2027… then we may see this project finished before Piston Peak if they do manage to re-use the existing attraction dome and infrastructure. At that point it comes down to gutting the old ride and installing the new roller coaster hardware inside the existing dome.
    While it is possible that they could try to re-build Space Mountain to be exactly like it is now, just with new track and trains… I’d consider that the less likely option as goals of this refurbishment would be to try and either increase the capacity of the attraction if possible, or cut back on the required staffing, and changing it over to be a single station and track layout like the California version would likely help with both of those items. As it is right now, the current layout of Space Mountain in Florida is very labor intensive, as it features not only two separate loading stations, but also two additional unload stations on the floor below. Compare this to the Disneyland version, where the larger two-wide train may have the same capacity as 2 of the WDW trains, but the attraction runs with a single load/unload station, requiring far less crewmembers to operate. Even if the new version had a split load/unload station like Big Thunder or Hyperspace Mountain in Paris, which use longer more high capacity trains, operations would still be more efficient than what the Magic Kingdom has now.

But look in the foreground where it appears that water is falling down into a strange black rocky abyss into the depths of the earth and you can clearly see more roller coaster track (and a train) spiraling down into the depths.
    Disney’s design team say that they’ve been touring Europe for design inspirations, which makes me wonder if they've dropped by Phantasialand in Germany, where that park’s highly rated Taron launched coaster could definitely serve as a piece of inspiration as to how Disney could possibly design their own Villain themed coaster. The way Taron weaves through a very creepy landscape of rocks is beautiful to see. While it is an Intamin designed launched coaster, it actually features no inversions of any kind.

 
    (11/21/2024) Disney’s Josh D’Amaro dropped an interesting new detail about the new planned Villains themed land in development for The Magic Kingdom theme park. During the D23 Brazil event, D’Amaro mentioned that the Villains land will be inspired by 13 specific villains after a “mysterious spell” was cast that summoned the various villains from their individual realms together in this new space. “In this land, heroes don’t come to save the day - and there may not be a happily ever after!”
    Sounding very much like the turbo-charged version of the cast of Fantasmic, the 13 gathered villains are:
Queen of Hearts (Alice in Wonderland),
Jafar (Aladdin)
Lady Tremaine (Cinderella)
Yzma (The Emperor’s New Groove)
Cruella de Vil (101 Dalmatians)
Captain Hook (Peter Pan)
Gaston (Beauty & The Beast)
Mother Gothel (Tangled)
Ursula (The Little Mermaid)
Hades (Hercules)
Chernabog (Fantasia)
Maleficent (Sleeping Beauty)
and the Evil Queen (Snow White & The Seven Dwarves)
    It’s not a bad list at all, bringing in most of the top tier baddies, though I wouldn’t mind seeing Lady Tremaine swapped out with someone a bit more sinister like Claude Frollo (Hunchback of Notre-Dame) or really Dr. Facilier (Princess and the Frog), especially after the good Doctor was completely overlooked for use on Tiana’s Bayou Adventure.
