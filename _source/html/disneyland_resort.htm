General Resort News - (11/11/2026) An interesting update was posted to MickeyVisit this week after Disney said they were going to be rolling out a new test experience app called Disney Imagineering Labs. Believe it or not, the goal of the new app is actually to try and reduce the amount of “screen time” for guests in the park that is taking them out of the true theme park experience. Yes… now it seems that Disney execs have finally realized that Disney guests are simply spending too much time on their phones when visiting the theme parks.
    I guess they shouldn’t have integrated virtually every park of the modern experience into guests phone then… from Lightning Lanes to mobile food ordering, maps with wait times, and restaurant reservations… a typical day at a Disney park is now seemly spent reserving every aspect of your experience on the app. So the solution… let’s try a better app?
    I’m not sure I buy it, as I’d have to experience it myself first hand, and to make the new app even stranger, apparently it can also work with Meta Ray-Ban Glasses, so you can keep your phone in your pocket and just access the app hands-free with your head instead. So are they really just trying to change the hardware you use to access the theme park app? Is the goal simply to transition guests to “hands-free” technology so they will be free to carry around the next obnoxiously huge themed pop-corn bucket, or gnaw on a massive turkey-leg without fear of getting fatty grease smeared on their iphone screen?
    To be, the best way to to be screen free is to get rid of all the reservation non-sense and apps and just let people walk through the parks and experience everything first hand, explore environments as they come to them, and just let everyone wait in the same line and get the same experience for the rides instead of worrying about who is skipping ahead in the queue because they bought a better tier of ticket. Keep your eyes sharp, because maybe the characters will be allowed to roam freely through the park again, instead of hiding as a photo op at the end of yet another queue. Make the experience spontaneous and “alive” again, and maybe guests won’t resort to pulling out their phones every couple of minutes, except to take pictures.
    Apparently some guests are being asked to test out an early version of the new app at Disneyland only right now, so if you’re a local passholder, maybe look into it and tell us what it is like.

    (5/24/2026) The Disneyland Resort has been slapped with a potential $5 million lawsuit this month over the theme park resort’s newly launched use of facial recognition technology in April. Given the rise of this kind of technology mixed with AI data analysis, this should prove to be an interesting case that could affect a lot of future projects. According to the lawsuit claim, Disney is accused of violating “privacy, competition and consumer protection laws” through the collection of the biometric data of their guests. It says that “disney doe snot adequately disclose the use of their biometric collection, so consumers - which almost always include children - have no idea that Disney is collecting this highly sensitive data”. They believe that rather than having guests actively select to opt-out of this data collection, which is the current policy, that Disney’s guests should automatically be excluded until they actively “opt-in” to use it.
    Of course, with that kind of thought and an opt-in bottleneck, then Disney would have never created and put this kind of technology to use in the first place. According to Disney however, with the exception of where data needs to be kept for legal or fraud-prevention purposes, Disneyland says that they delete all numerical values collected within 30 days of creation. Guests are also able to opt-out of the facial scan process by using clearly marked entrance lanes into the parks that are marked as such. Any pictures of your face done in these lanes are not collected by their biometric system and instead a cast member will manually validate your ticket to enter the parks.
    This does remind me of a similar class action lawsuit that was filed against Six Flags Great America in Illinois back in 2016 by a family angry about finger scanners being used at the theme park to verify the identity of their annual passholders. While this was a process used at other Six Flags parks as well as being a common practice at most major theme parks in the Orlando area for many years by then, the state of Illinois had passed a specific law (Illinois Biometric Privacy Law) in 2008 that required entities to have written consent from a person before collecting and storing their biometric information. In this case, the family claimed that their son (and others) were tasked to provide his fingerprint without giving consent. The case went all the way up to the state’s Supreme Court to consider if there was any actual injury or harm that took place in the process, rather than just the gathering of data without consent. Six Flags argument was to claim no injury was caused because the data collected was neither breached, stolen, or used in any way other than as intended by Six Flags, but in the end Six Flags opted to a $36 million settlement agreement to end the class-action lawsuit without admitting to any fault or liability in 2021.

 
    (5/2/2026) According to this article, the Disneyland Resort is updating some of their rules as a reflection of guest behavior issues. The first is a technology trend that we’ve seen as something to expect to be incoming for several years now, as it has already been rolled out at a number of international parks as well as several Orlando area parks. This would be the addition to facial ID scanning for guests as they enter the Disneyland parks. As you enter a camera will scan your face and if this is your first entry, it will record the digital measurement of your features as data that it will compare to future images of you when you re-enter one of the parks. This is an effort to crackdown on attempts to share both day-pass and annual passes. This can be done more quickly by a computer than wanting for an employee at the gate to visually compare you to the picture on file that pops up on a tiny little screen. For those who are reluctant to undergo this kind of processing, you can opt out and ask to be verified by a human. Much like opting out of the scanner at the airport, this will take you a bit longer to get through the process and into the park however.
    The other big change at Disneyland that has started is a bit of a bigger one to get used to, as Disneyland will now begin to ask guests to put their phones away in a secure bag or pocket when riding most attractions. While this was previously done mostly on attractions that were entirely screen / media based (like Star Tours or Soarin’), Disney will now be asking everyone to “Stow It, Don’t Show It” in regards to phones as well as any other large and loose belongings, including all those large drink cups, souvenir popcorn containers, etc…

 

Ticket Pricing - (10/7/2026) Ticket prices at Disneyland just went up this week… but this time around, the change is a little different. The lowest and the highest tier pricing to visit either of the Disneyland Resort theme parks for a single day is actually staying the same, with Tier 0 starting at $104 and Tier 6 (the highest) capping off at $224 a day. The actual increases are instead taking place to most, but not all, of the tiers in between, with several bumping up about $5. I believe the cost to add Park Hopping to your pass has also increased, starting at $75 and goes up for those with multi-day passes.

    (10/8/2025) Disney has raised ticket prices at Disneyland and Walt Disney World this week. The parks play the “tier” game by keeping prices for the few calendar days on the lowest tier about the same as before while increasing the price to get in on the other tiers. The low price tier at Disneyland will be $104 for a one day admission, but if you drop in on a busy day, the top tier price will have increased up to $224, an increase of $18.

    (3/27/21) The Disneyland Resort surprised everyone with the announcement of a new future initiative and growth plan called Disneyland Forward. According to Disney, the last time they established the future growth plans of the Disneyland Resort was 25 years ago, back when there was only one theme park at the resort and Disney California Adventure was still just a dream.
    Back then the plans they set with the City of Anaheim established various “districts” or zones if you will, that would only allow for the development of various things in specific places. Parking here, shopping there, new hotels way over there, and so on. 25 years later, Disney now feels that they have reached the limit of those plans, which are now restricting future concepts of growth now expected from more current and integrated theme park resort concepts. As such, Disney would like to explore new growth opportunities that could see mixed use concepts arise in all new locations going forward.
    As Disney well put it, “Without broadening the uses allowed within each district or demolishing and replacing many beloved theme park attractions, further integrated development and theme park investment are not possible.”
    While nothing is set in stone yet, the artwork examples Disney is showing explores the possibility of expanding both Disneyland and Disney California Adventure to plots of land on the other side of Disneyland Drive, to the north and south of the Disneyland hotels that are currently used only for parking. Much like how foot traffic crosses over Disneyland Drive via a massive and well landscaped foot bridge that makes it just look like a regular pathway to the guests. Similar connecting pathways could be constructed to link up the theme parks to these parking lots, likely branching off from the backside of Critter Country inside Disneyland, and from the west side Paradise Pier somewhere between Inside Out Emotional Whirl and Boardwalk Pizza inside California Adventure.
    The former parking lots would be transformed into all new theme park lands for each park. Again,  no formal plans have been made, but the Disneyland Forward website does mention concepts like adding lands and attractions themed to Frozen, Tangled and Peter Pan to Disneyland (similar to plans now under way at Tokyo Disneysea) and adding other attractions like the new Zootopia land (under construction at Disneyland Shanghai), the TRON coaster (Shanghai and under construction at Walt Disney World) and a Toy Story Land (like the one at Walt Disney World) to Disney California Adventure.
    The future plans also include ideas like adding a mini Disney Springs style retail and dining area to the land Disney owns South of the theme parks on Harbor and Katella, but this version would also integrate a brand new Disney Resort hotel into the complex as well. It also looks like DIsneyland is ready to revive their previous plans they put on hold to build another parking structure on the other side of Harbor that would lead guests over Harbor via another large footbridge and directly into the esplanade between the entrances to the two theme parks.
    Based on past Disney history, you might think that the Disneyland Resort would be seeking some kind of tax incentive deal from Anaheim for all this future growth, but actually the truth couldn’t be further. Just as we saw Disneyland tell Anaheim that they were requesting the city to cancel two of their existing Tax Subsidies (a 20-year 70% hotel tax rebate and a 45-year exemption to taxes on theme park tickets) because they felt the existing deals from a previous political climate had become increasingly “divisive” and had made for a “difficult working relationship” with the current city of Anaheim.
    According to Disney’s own statement, “We are at the very beginning stages of the process. While the project will be refined over time, we hope to explore the creation of integrated experiences featuring new theme park attractions, dining, retail, hotel, and more. Right now, we don’t have any specific projects planned for the future. To be clear, Disney is not seeking any public funding for DisneylandForward, nor are we seeking additional square footage or hotel rooms beyond what is currently approved and allowed. Rather, we are simply asking to update our existing approvals to allow for integrated development to be located and built on Disney properties.”
    The timing of this announcement is especially interesting, as the California resort is now preparing to finally reopen to guests at the end of April after being closed due to COVID-19 for just over a year’s time. The fact that Disney is coming forward with these huge growth plans is a great sign that they feel very confident in the future growth potential of their California property, to say nothing of the huge potential for job growth from the planning, construction and staffing of these expansions going forward.
