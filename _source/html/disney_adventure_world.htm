    (12/29/2025) Disney is once again testing the waters about removing 3D content and glasses from one of their attractions in Paris. If Screamscape readers recall, Disney quietly tested guest reactions to the removal of the 3D glasses on the Ratatouille dark ride in Walt Disney Studios Paris for a short time back in 2019 only to put it back, and never really mentioned why. Further tests were quietly conducted here and there over the next few years, but in 2025 Disney confirmed that they were going to completely renovate the attraction, permanently removing the 3D elements and enhancing many of the scenes and updating the projection content for the permanent 2D conversion when it reopens in 2026. The same conversion was also confirmed to be coming to the Epcot version of Ratatouille as well.
    The removal of 3D content, where it isn’t really needed, has been ongoing trend I’ve been watching unfold for many years now. If you are curious, I wrote a detailed article about the trend that was posted by Blooloop in October that is worth a quick read! (Many don’t remember that Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey in Hollywood and Japan operated with 3D glasses for a time!)
    So what’s changing in Paris today you ask? Without any explanation, Disney was seen testing operations of the Spider-Man W.E.B. Adventure dark ride on December 26th without 3D glasses and even posted a sign out front notifying guests of the change. (See Tweet below)
    So does this mean we can expect to see the 3D removed from Spider-Man in a few years? Maybe… maybe not. The conversion of Spider-Man into a 2D attraction may not be as simple as you might think, due to the interactive nature of the ride system. At its core,  W.E.B. Adventure, really is a giant video game, with about every scene represented by giant screens. This means the content and entire scenes can easily be swapped out simply by replacing whatever is on the screen with something else entirely. We’ve seen Disney do a time or two over the years with their Toy Story Midway Mania attractions, quietly replacing the content of a scene with a new one in order to add characters new newer Toy Story films into the attraction.
    However as the game-play with Spider-Man uses sensors to detect hand-motions by the riders, instead of aiming a blaster or pop-gun, the system is a little more complicated. While I can’t confirm exactly how it all works, the guests “shooting webs” from the confines of the ride vehicles actually works fairly well, and I would think that the sensors may be detecting just how hard or forceful they are moving their hands forward toward the screen to possibly determine if they are shooting webs at a targets that are close or far away. The use of the 3D glasses and projection system would then be a deciding factor for the riders, unconsciously, to be putting a little more “force” into their movements when trying to shoot webs at targets that they view as being further away.
    While the same thing would still happen in a 2D version of the ride, allowing it to function, there would likely have to be some recalibration of the software to adjust the aiming system for new movement patterns. So this is potentially what occasional “tests” like what was done the other day may also be for, in addition to just getting a feel from guests about how they enjoy the attraction minus the headgear. So I think it’s safe to say that some possible changes may be coming… just maybe not for another year or two, while they work things out and maybe find some other ways to improve the guest experience.

    (8/30/2025) Disney has tested this in years past to gauge guest reactions, but Blooloop reports that this October the Walt Disney Studios Paris theme park will close their Ratatouille dark ride for a major refurbishment that will also feature the removal of the 3D projection system when it reopens in Spring 2026.
    As part of the renovation the attraction will receive a number of technical upgrades as well as some scenic enhancements that will allow for a more “immersive experience” when it reopens sans-3D next year. This will include some new themed additions to the queue, new projection enhancement within the ride itself to take advantage of the wider view guests will have without the glasses, as well as new and updates set pieces that will add to the perception that you’ve been shrunk down to rat-size to fit in with Remy and his family.
    Disney also confirms that all of the projection tech will be upgraded to current state-of-the-art systems, because if you recall this original version of the attraction actually opened way back in 2014. On a technical side, you will likely find that the various projection scenes will be very clear and bright, as 3D projection requires two image projectors to blend an image that the glasses then filter so that each eye only sees one of the images, creating the 3D effect within your brain. Removing the dark filtered 3D glasses from the equation (which dim the image) will allow for a brighter more clear image that everyone can see, as well as removing any slight imperfections from the visuals as there is no longer a need to calibrate and overlap the dueling 3D projections footage, which also results in a softer image.
    Another pleasant side effect of this is that those guests who find that moving 3D images on rides can make them feel a little motion-sick, should find this new 2D version of Ratatoulle much less stomach-churning. A fun little story… while Universal’s original Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey ride in Orlando has always been presented in 2D, Universal did test out a 3D version in Japan and Hollywood for a time when those later versions opened. Guests did not react well to the more dizzying effect from the 3D and they switched those versions over to 2D like the Orlando original.
